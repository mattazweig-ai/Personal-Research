# TIF & Value Capture — DFW Lens

A durable primer on the mechanics that move land value in the deals we track.
Written to be reused, not just for the arena deal.

## 1. Tax Increment Financing (TIF) — how it works

- A city draws a boundary (the **TIF district / TIRZ** in Texas — Tax Increment
  Reinvestment Zone) and freezes the assessed value at a **base year**.
- As redevelopment lifts assessed value, the property taxes on the *increment* (value
  above base) are diverted into a **TIF fund** instead of the general budget.
- That fund pays for **public infrastructure** inside the zone (roads, utilities,
  parks, parking, sometimes land/demolition) — often reimbursing the developer who
  fronts it. TIRZ in TX have a statutory life (commonly ~20–30 yrs) and a cap.
- **Why developers love it:** it's a way to get public infrastructure paid out of the
  value *the project itself creates*, without a general tax increase. The increment is
  the subsidy — paid in tax-base growth, not cash up front.

**Investor takeaways:**
- TIF presence signals the city has *already* committed to enabling density/value here.
- But TIF is **policy, not contract** — boundaries, caps, term, and allocations can be
  amended by the TIF board / council. Underwrite the *plan language and remaining term*,
  not the press release.

## 2. The "barbell" structure (Dallas's twist)

Dallas linked **two TIRZ** — the affluent **Dallas Midtown / Valley View** north node
and **Redbird / southern Dallas** — so increment generated at the strong end can be
**transferred to subsidize the weak end** (reporting cites $100M+ toward Redbird).

- **Equity rationale:** north-Dallas value growth funds south-Dallas revitalization.
- **Investor angle:** an anchor (arena) that supercharges the north node *mechanically
  enlarges the pool flowing south* → Redbird land is a leveraged, under-watched
  beneficiary. (Highest asymmetry, highest political/execution risk. See deal doc §4.)
- **Risk:** transfers are the first thing to get politically renegotiated. Don't treat
  the barbell as a guaranteed flow.

## 3. Anchor-tenant value capture — the general model

The pattern across modern stadium/arena districts (and corporate campuses):

1. A dead or underused asset (mall, brownfield) sits on a large, contiguous, well-located
   parcel — **the scarce ingredient is assembled, entitled-able land near highways.**
2. An **anchor** (sports venue, HQ, experiential retail) supplies the demand certainty
   that mixed-use (resi/hotel/office/retail) needs to finance.
3. The **landowner/anchor owner captures the residual land uplift** — often the biggest
   profit pool, larger than operating the venue itself.
4. The public typically contributes via **land/entitlement concessions and TIF**, not
   (in the privately-financed cases) direct construction cash.

**Comp — The Battery Atlanta (Truist Park):** on-site parcels ~$5M (2014) → ~$644M
(2025); ~$4.5B surrounding "halo" lift. Caveat: academic analyses dispute whether the
*public's* return justified the subsidy — value creation ≠ taxpayer win. Succeeded with
**no rail transit** (highway + parking model) — relevant to car-dependent DFW.

## 4. Where the outside investor actually plays

You usually can't buy the anchor parcel. The accessible surface:

- **Halo adjacencies** — resi/SFR and retail/hotel pads within ~1–2 mi of the anchor.
- **The leveraged public-side beneficiary** — e.g., the TIF-barbell's southern node.
- **Liquid proxies** — public companies whose value tracks the play (e.g., LVS for the
  Adelson/Dumont Texas optionality; mall REITs holding redevelopment-candidate sites).
- **Parallel plays** — the same pattern repeating elsewhere in the metro (Stars → Plano
  Willow Bend is a mall-to-arena-district mirror in Collin County).

## 5. Quick checklist for any DFW anchor/redevelopment deal

- [ ] Who holds the dirt and at what basis? (Patient long-hold = big uplift leverage.)
- [ ] Is it an **option/LOI** or a **closed/contracted** deal?
- [ ] Is there a **TIRZ**? What's its base year, cap, remaining term, and any transfers?
- [ ] **Financing:** private, public, or mixed? (Private = conviction + lower political risk.)
- [ ] **Entitlements:** zoned for the intended density/use yet, or PD changes needed?
- [ ] **Access:** highway/parking sufficient? (DFW rewards car access over rail.)
- [ ] **Comparable** completed district to anchor magnitude expectations (Battery, etc.).
- [ ] What **instrument can we actually touch**, and what's the cleanest risk-adjusted one?
