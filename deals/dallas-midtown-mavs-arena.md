# Mavericks Arena @ Valley View / "Dallas Midtown"

**Site:** ~104 acres, Preston Rd & I-635 (LBJ), Far North Dallas (~10–12 mi N of downtown)
**Announced:** 2026-06-01 — Mavs signed **option agreements** (not a closed purchase)
**Status:** Pre-entitlement. No renderings, no published cost, no confirmed financing mix.
**Target:** ~30-month build, open by end of 2031 / 2031–32 season (AAC lease expires July 2031).

---

## 1. The one-paragraph thesis

The Mavericks (controlled by the Adelson/Dumont family, who also run Las Vegas Sands)
are taking an **option** on ~104 acres at the dead Valley View Mall to anchor a new
arena + entertainment district. The land is the long-stalled "Dallas Midtown" assembly
owned by **Beck Ventures**. The arena is the anchor that finally makes Beck's 14-year,
~$4B vision pencil. This is, first and foremost, a **real-estate value-capture play**
dressed as a sports story — the durable money is in the land and the development rights
around the arena, and in the TIF that recycles the new tax base back into infrastructure.
The hard unknowns (financing mix, entitlements, transit, a still-open Mavs–Stars legal
fight) are all about *who* captures the upside and *how much* leaks to the public side.

## 2. Why this is a real-estate deal, not a sports deal

The single most important frame (per CNN's reporting on the broader trend): **owners now
make more from the development rights around a venue than from the venue itself.** Public
financing for arenas increasingly takes the form of *land and entitlement concessions* to
the owner rather than cash. So the right question is never "is the arena a good deal for
the city" — it's **"who ends up owning the appreciating dirt, and on what terms."**

Here that splits three ways:

| Party | What they control | How they capture value |
|---|---|---|
| **Beck Ventures** | The ~110-acre master assembly + master-developer role | Land basis from ~2012–13; arena de-risks the rest of the pad, lifts residual land value, anchors retail/hotel/office rents |
| **Mavs / Sands (Adelson–Dumont)** | Option on ~104 acres + the arena/PSL/premium business | Arena economics + their own slice of mixed-use; optionality on the land while financing/entitlements resolve |
| **City of Dallas** | Zoning, the TIF, a 20-acre city-owned "Midtown Commons" park | New tax increment (recycled), the barbell transfer to southern Dallas, civic anchor |

**Pushback on the "Beck got bailed out" read:** partly true, but the option structure
means Beck monetizes *optionality and certainty*, not a clean exit. The anchor tenant
that institutional capital, hotel flags, and condo buyers were all waiting for just
showed up. That's worth far more to the residual land than a single 104-acre sale price.

## 3. The comp that matters: The Battery Atlanta

The closest structural comp to a suburban, privately-led, owner-developed arena district:

- Truist Park / The Battery (Cobb County, opened 2017), Atlanta Braves.
- Parcel taxable value at the site went from **~$5M (2014, 4 parcels)** to **~$644M
  (2025, 60 parcels)** — and a broader **"halo" of ~$4.5B** in surrounding value lift
  since the 2013 announcement.
- Generated tens of millions/yr in tax revenue for the county/schools.

**Read-through to Valley View:** the magnitude of on-site value creation is enormous and
real — *for whoever owns the parcels*. That's Beck and the Mavs/Sands, not the public and
not an outside passive investor. The halo effect is what reaches adjacent owners (Galleria,
Preston/Tollway frontage, nearby multifamily and SFR). **Caveat:** academic work on the
Battery is split on whether the *public's* return justified the subsidy — a useful warning
that "values went up" and "the deal was good for taxpayers" are different claims.

## 4. The TIF "barbell" — the under-covered angle

Dallas structured **two linked TIF districts — "Dallas Midtown" (Valley View) and
"Valley View/Mall Area Redevelopment"** — deliberately tied to **Redbird / southern
Dallas** so that increment generated at the wealthy north end can fund the south end.
Reporting points to **$100M+** of increment potentially routed toward Redbird.

Why this matters to us:
- An arena anchor dramatically raises the *velocity and ceiling* of increment at the
  north node — which mechanically increases the pool flowing to the southern node.
- **Southern Dallas / Redbird is the asymmetric, under-priced adjacency.** Everyone is
  staring at Preston & LBJ; far fewer are connecting the arena to a funded uplift in
  Redbird land. If the barbell holds politically, that's where mispricing is most likely.
- *Risk:* TIF mechanics are political. Increment can be reallocated, capped, or slowed;
  the barbell is a policy promise, not a contract you can underwrite. Verify the actual
  TIF plan language and remaining term before leaning on it.

## 5. Financing: the central unknown (and why "private" is bullish)

- Welts built the Warriors' **Chase Center — privately financed (~$1.4B)** — and was
  hired by Dumont (Jan 2025) specifically to run this. The Mavs are signaling a
  **private, PSL/premium-funded** model in that mold.
- **Why private financing is bullish for nearby values:** (a) it signals owner
  conviction and balance-sheet commitment; (b) it sidesteps the public-subsidy political
  drag that can stall or kill deals (see the Irving casino backlash); (c) Sands has the
  capital depth to actually deliver, lowering execution risk.
- **But it is NOT confirmed.** No published cost, no public/private split. Treat any
  "private" assumption as a hypothesis until a term sheet or city agreement surfaces.

## 6. Transit gap — pushing back on the loudest criticism

Consensus criticism: no DART rail at Valley View (buses only); a NCTCOG-funded "people
mover" from the **Silverline** is only floated.

**My pushback:** the strongest suburban arena-district comp — The Battery — succeeded
with **no rail transit at all**, on a highway-and-parking model, in a metro that drives.
DFW is even more car-dependent. The site sits at the junction of **I-635 + Dallas North
Tollway + Preston**, which is exactly the access Cuban praised. Transit absence is a
real friction for game-day egress and for the "walkable district" pitch, but on the
evidence it is **not disqualifying** for value creation. Don't over-weight it.
*Where it does bite:* the residential/office "live-here" premium and ESG/transit-oriented
narratives — and any people-mover capex becomes a public-cost line to watch.

## 7. Where can *we* actually invest? (the honest part)

We cannot buy into the 104 acres — Beck and Sands have it. So the actionable surface for
an individual/family investor is the **adjacencies and the public proxies**:

1. **Las Vegas Sands (NYSE: LVS) — the cleanest public proxy.** Same family
   (Adelson/Dumont) controls the Mavs *and* LVS; Dumont became LVS chairman/CEO
   (Mar 1, 2026). LVS also holds **~1,000 acres near old Texas Stadium in Irving** as
   a casino-resort land bank — i.e., a *second* DFW value-capture call option. LVS is a
   liquid way to ride the family's Texas real-estate + gaming optionality. **Caveats:**
   LVS's value is dominated by Macau/Singapore/Vegas, so the Texas optionality is a thin
   slice of the story; the Mavs themselves are privately held and not in LVS. Not advice —
   size it as a thematic tilt, not a pure-play.
2. **Adjacent residential** — condos/townhomes and SFR within the halo (Preston Hollow
   edge, Galleria/Far North Dallas, near the ~2-mi **Netflix House at Galleria**). Halo
   appreciation is the retail-investor's version of the Battery uplift.
3. **Redbird / southern Dallas land** — the TIF-barbell beneficiary almost nobody is
   pricing. Highest asymmetry, highest execution/political risk. Long horizon.
4. **Plano / Collin County (the Stars spillover)** — see §8; a *parallel* arena-district
   land thesis at Willow Bend, separate ownership/politics.
5. **Hospitality / flagged-hotel and retail pads** along Preston & the Tollway frontage
   that feed off arena traffic — for those with operating capability.

## 8. Adjacent threads (track, don't trade yet)

- **Stars → Plano (Willow Bend).** Plano in talks / informal LOI for a ~75-acre,
  **~$1B** arena-district at the dying Shops at Willow Bend; decision possibly within
  6–8 months, build 2028–29 for 2031–32. Mavs–Stars are in an **unprecedented legal
  fight** over the AAC and are splitting up. Downtown could lose **both** anchors.
  → A second mall-to-arena-district redevelopment in DFW, in fast-growing Collin County.
- **Casino long-game.** Gaming is **illegal in TX**; legalization died again in 2025
  (Patrick/Senate). Next shot: **2027 session**. Sands is funding a ~$9M PAC for 2026
  elections and hiring casino-software roles in DFW. Notably the Mavs chose **Valley
  View, NOT Sands' Irving casino land** — signaling the arena and the casino are being
  run as *separate* options, not a single Irving mega-bet.
- **Victory Park / downtown.** Goldman Sachs ~$709M campus (5,000+ jobs) is under
  construction by the AAC — downtown's anchor base survives the teams' exit.

## 9. Risk register

| Risk | Why it matters | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Option ≠ close | Mavs can walk; nothing is contractually permanent | Closing announcement / land transfer |
| Entitlements/zoning | Not yet approved; arena traffic + height need PD changes | City Plan Commission / Council votes |
| Financing mix | Public subsidy fight could stall it (cf. Irving) | Term sheet, city development agreement |
| TIF reallocation | Barbell to Redbird is policy, not contract | TIF board actions, plan amendments |
| Transit/egress | Game-day flow; "people mover" capex is public cost | NCTCOG funding decisions |
| Mavs–Stars litigation | Could reshape timing/economics of both moves | Court filings, settlement |
| Macro (rates) | Vertical mixed-use needs cheap capital to pencil | Construction starts beyond The Premier |
| 2026/2027 elections | Casino + any public funding ride on these | TX legislature, local races |

## 10. Bottom line

This is a textbook **anchor-tenant value-capture** play. The asymmetric, less-crowded
angles are **(a)** the Redbird/southern-Dallas TIF-barbell beneficiary, and **(b)** LVS
as the liquid family/Texas-optionality proxy. The most over-weighted bear point is the
transit gap — the best comp (Battery) says highway access is enough to create value.
The genuine swing factors are **financing disclosure** and **whether the option closes
and gets entitled.** Until those resolve, this is a thesis to *position around the edges
of*, not to chase at the center.
