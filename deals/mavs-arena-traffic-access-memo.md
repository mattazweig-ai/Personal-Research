## 1. Direct answer: is road expansion planned? — **No funded expansion at the site.**

| Facility | Status near the site | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **I-635 (LBJ Express)** | **Already rebuilt 2015** (~$2.6B P3): 4 general-purpose + up to 3 tolled managed + 2–3 frontage lanes **each way**. | This *is* the built condition. No further widening funded here. |
| **LBJ East ($1.74B, under construction)** | Limits **US-75 → I-30**, east of the High Five | **Does NOT reach** Preston/DNT. Irrelevant to site access. |
| **Dallas North Tollway** | No funded widening/ramp work near LBJ/Spring Valley (active DNT widening is in Frisco, ~20 mi N) | No relief here. |
| **I-635/DNT interchange & High Five** | Complete (2015 / 2005). No reconstruction. | No relief here. |
| **Montfort Drive** | **Just put on a road DIET: 6 → 4 lanes** + bike lanes (~$4.5M + ~$5.4M, ~2024) | Capacity *reduced* on one adjacent arterial. Minor facility, but the direction of travel is notable. |
| **People mover (DART connector)** | ~2.2-mi elevated ATS, ~$95M; **only ~$10M planning money committed** — construction unfunded | The "funded by NCTCOG" framing is overstated — it's an adopted plan seeking grants. |
| **DART Silverline rail** | **Preston & Coit stations were CANCELLED/never built**; nearest = Knoll Trail (DNT/Arapaho) & Addison | No adjacent rail. Auto mode share will be high. |
| **Internal street grid (PD 887)** | Conceptual/developer-led; Seritage building some private roads | Local circulation, not capacity for event egress. |

**Bottom line:** as of today the arena would be dropped onto **essentially the 2015 network**,
with one adjacent arterial slightly *narrowed*, and no committed highway, ramp, or rail
capacity addition. **No arena-specific Traffic Impact Analysis (TIA) exists yet** — expected,
since this is still an option, pre-entitlement.

## 2. Demand: what an NBA event puts on the network (benchmark estimate)

Assumptions (stated): ~19,000 attendees; auto mode share **~88%** (weak transit → high);
avg vehicle occupancy **~2.5**.
- **Inbound vehicles ≈ 19,000 × 0.88 / 2.5 ≈ ~6,700 vehicles** per event.
- **Parking need ≈ 5,000–7,000 spaces** — consistent with auto-oriented comps at
  **~0.25–0.35 spaces/seat** (United Center ~6,000; AAC >5,000; Fiserv ~4,400; Crypto 3,300
  on-site). Contrast Chase Center (transit-first) at **950 spaces ≈ 0.05/seat** — *not*
  achievable here without rail.

**Implication:** on a dense ~104-ac district, 5,000–7,000 spaces means **structured parking**
(surface would eat ~50+ acres). That's major capex and it competes with developable land —
a real pro-forma drag, and part of why the arena *needs* the surrounding district revenue.

## 3. The binding constraint is EGRESS, not daily traffic

Daily AADT on I-635/DNT/Preston is large but irrelevant to the real risk. The problem is the
**post-event surge**: ~6,700 vehicles trying to leave in ~30–45 min ≈ a peak demand on the
order of **~9,000–13,000 vph** hitting garage exits, frontage roads, ramps, and signals.

- Freeway *mainline* capacity is ample (8 GP lanes + managed on I-635 alone ≈ 16,000+ vph
  theoretical). **The jam is never the mainline** — it's **garage egress points → frontage
  road weave → ramp junctions → arterial signals.** That's where every arena backs up.

## 4. The honest verdict: manageable engineering problem, not a fatal flaw — but unfunded

**Why it's survivable (the bull case):**
- **Three limited-access facilities converge here** (I-635, DNT, plus Preston arterial) —
  *more egress directions than most downtown sites,* including the AAC. Multi-directional
  dispersal is exactly what egress engineering wants.
- **The comps prove it works:** United Center (20,900 seats, auto-oriented, freeway-fed) and
  The Battery/Truist Park (41,000, fed by I-75/I-285/US-41) discharge fine with active
  traffic management (police-controlled signals, ITS message boards, timed garage release,
  rideshare staging). Cuban's "accessible from 635 and the tollway" is engineering-defensible.

**Why your worry is legitimate (the bear case):**
- **Nobody has funded the egress engineering** — ramp/garage-exit design, signal/ITS
  upgrades, possible new direct ramps to I-635/DNT, and the people mover are all **unfunded
  today.** These normally fall out of the **TIA during entitlements** and become a
  **public-funding ask** (TxDOT/city/TIF). Expect a number — and a political fight — later.
- **High auto mode share** (no rail) means the network must absorb ~6,700 vehicles with
  little transit offload. Chase Center solves egress with Muni; this site can't, yet.
- The **Montfort road diet** signals the city's recent arterial posture has been
  capacity-*reducing* (complete-streets), which sits in tension with arena surge needs.

## 5. Investor translation
- **Traffic is a cost/entitlement risk, not a kill-shot.** It won't sink the value thesis,
  but it will (a) require expensive structured parking that competes with developable land,
  and (b) generate a future **public infrastructure ask** (ramps/ITS/people mover) — a line
  item and a negotiating lever, not a solved problem.
- **The catalyst to watch is the TIA in the entitlement phase.** That document will reveal
  the real parking count, mitigation scope, and who pays. Until then, "good freeway access"
  is true but **incomplete** — the egress/ramp engineering is the unpriced piece.
- Net: keep traffic in the **risk register as a cost & timing item**, not as a reason the
  arena fails. My earlier "transit gap is overweighted as a kill-shot" call **stands** — but
  the *parking capex* and the *unfunded mitigation* are the parts the boosters gloss over.
